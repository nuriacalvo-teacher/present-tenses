Chapter 01 · Routines, facts & states

Present simple

he / she / it+verb + s

Routines, habits, general truths, timetables and states.

ESme levanto a las siete · el agua se congela a 0 °C

Routine · general truth
I get up at seven every day.
Water freezes at zero degrees.
He · she · it → add -s
My sister play the piano.
My sister plays the piano.
do / does → no -s
Tom doesn’t likes coffee.
Tom doesn’t like coffee. · Does she live here?
always · usually · never
I always read at night.
She is never late.
Chapter 02 · Happening now

Present continuous

am / is / are+verb-ing

Actions in progress: right now, these days, and fixed plans.

ESestá lloviendo · estos días estoy leyendo un libro

Right now
Look! It’s raining!
Temporary · these days
I’m reading a great book these days.
Fixed plan · change
We’re flying to Rome on Friday.
The weather is getting warmer.
Don’t forget be!
She working now.
She is working now.
Chapter 03 · The past that matters now

Present perfect

have / has+past participle

It connects the past with the present. We never say when.

EShe estado en Londres · he perdido las llaves

Experience · ever / never
Have you ever eaten sushi?
Result now · just
I’ve lost my keys!
Your mum has just arrived.
Up to now · for / since
We’ve lived here for ten years.
I’ve known her since 2019.
has been to Paris → and came back · has gone to Paris → still there
Finished time → past simple
I have seen him yesterday.
I saw him yesterday.
Chapter 04 · How long?

Present perfect continuous

have / has+been+verb-ing

How long an activity has been going on — or an activity with a visible effect.

ESllevo seis años estudiando inglés

Duration · for / since / how long
I’ve been studying English for six years.
Visible effect
Your hands are dirty! What have you been doing?
Quantity → perfect simple
We have been writing five emails.
We have written five emails.
State verbs → no continuous
I have been knowing her for years.
I have known her for years.
Chapter 05 · Which one?

Simple or continuous?

The trick: look at the time words.
every day · usually · never → simple
I usually get up at seven.
now · at the moment · Look! → continuous
This week I’m getting up at nine.
ever · already · yet · just → perfect
We’ve seen this film before.
how long · for · since → perfect continuous
It’s been raining since Monday.
yesterday · last week · ago → none of the presents: past simple! I visited my gran last weekend.
State verbs (like, know, want) stay simple: She doesn’t like chocolate, but she’s eating a piece now.
Chapter 06 · Top mistakes

Spot the mistake

The five mistakes that cost you points in the exam.

-sam / is / arestate verbsparticipleyesterday

ESrepasa siempre esto antes de entregar el examen

1 · Forgetting the -s
He work in a bank.
He works in a bank.
2 · Forgetting be
They playing football now.
They are playing football now.
3 · State verbs in -ing
I am wanting a new phone.
I want a new phone.
4 · Past instead of participle
I have saw that film.
I have seen that film.
5 · A hidden yesterday
I have gone to the cinema yesterday.
I went to the cinema yesterday.
